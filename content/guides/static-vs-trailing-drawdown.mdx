## Understanding Drawdown Floor Mechanics

Static drawdowns lock your breach threshold relative to your starting balance, whereas trailing drawdowns ratchets the breach floor higher as your peak equity climbs.
This structural distinction alters risk exposure significantly as account equity grows over time.

## Static Drawdown: Fixed Risk Boundaries

Under a static drawdown rule, earning profit increases your buffer without raising your breach floor.
Your maximum allowable loss remains constant from the day the account is provisioned.

<Callout variant="framework" title="Rule Distinction">
Static drawdowns allow profit to cushion future losses, whereas trailing drawdowns lock in gains and narrow your remaining buffer as equity rises.
</Callout>

## Trailing Drawdown: The High Water Mark Trap

Trailing drawdowns move your termination floor upwards whenever a new equity high water mark is achieved.
If equity subsequently declines, the breach floor does not adjust downwards, creating a shrinking loss allowance.

## Key Verification Checks Before Trading

Always verify whether trailing drawdowns are calculated intraday or at end-of-day (EOD) in your firm's T&Cs.
Intraday trailing drawdowns measure floating equity peaks, while EOD models only lock in closed daily balances.
